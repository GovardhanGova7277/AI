### 5.  Summary  

- **Model Breadth:** Tata is building a diversified Indian‑centric lineup (Nexon, Tigor, Curvv, Sierra, Harrier, Tiago) covering sub‑compact to premium SUVs. BYD’s portfolio (Seal, Dolphin, Sealion, plus its broader range) targets both premium and mass‑market segments globally.  
- **Battery Strategy:** Tata is transitioning from imported cells to a locally‑sourced battery ecosystem, anchored by a $1.6 bn plant deal. BYD continues to own the entire battery value chain, using its Blade‑battery technology to drive cost and performance advantages.  
- **Competitive Edge:** Tata’s advantage lies in deep local market knowledge, government alignment, and a growing dealer network. BYD’s advantage stems from vertical integration, economies of scale, and a proven ability to launch competitively priced EVs worldwide.  

Both manufacturers are pursuing growth, but their strategic levers differ: Tata bets on **localization** and **segment breadth** within India, while BYD bets on **global vertical integration** and **price‑performance leadership**.
